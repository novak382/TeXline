\section{Equation of motion}
A mass $m$ on a spring of stiffness $k$ with viscous damping $c$
obeys $m\ddot x+c\dot x+kx=0$.
Assume $m>0$, $k>0$, and $c\geq0$.
Set $\gamma=c/(2m)$ and $\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}$.
\section{Underdamped response}
For $0\leq\gamma<\omega_0$, let
$\omega_d=\sqrt{\omega_0^2-\gamma^2}$.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solution}
 x(t)=A e^{-\gamma t}\cos(\omega_d t+\phi).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:solution} separates the decaying amplitude
from the oscillation frequency.
\section{Energy balance}
With $\mathcal E=m\dot x^2/2+kx^2/2$, direct differentiation gives
\[\frac{d\mathcal E}{dt}=-c\dot x^2\leq0.\]
The mechanical energy is therefore nonincreasing.
In the absence of damping, the same calculation recovers energy conservation.
